01 / IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

Build a modern farm in stages

Do not buy every gadget at once. Start with the cheapest measurement that changes a decision. Keep records, compare the results with your baseline, then invest in automation only when the payback is credible.

Measure first

Soil lab test, water availability, plot map, field photos, crop history and actual cost records.

Start with low-cost tools

Phone spreadsheet, local weather forecast, rain gauge, measuring container, pressure gauge and manual root-zone checks.

Pilot one technology

Trial moisture sensor, drip zone, pest trap or digital log on a manageable area; compare it with a similar baseline area where feasible.

Scale on evidence

Measure water, labour, input use, saleable yield, quality, rejections and net return across a meaningful crop cycle.

02 / TECHNOLOGY CATALOGUE

Tools, use process and sourcing

Costs vary by farm size, crop, installation, service, freight, connectivity and subsidy eligibility. Treat displayed prices as indicative listings, not a quote for your farm. Request a written, itemised quote and compare after-sales support before purchase.

LOW COST / FIRST PURCHASE

Soil lab test + digital field diary

Purpose: Avoid guessed nutrient inputs and make costs visible. A laboratory soil test is more decision-useful than a cheap multipurpose probe claiming to measure everything.

  • How: sample as instructed → submit to a recognised lab → ask an officer to interpret the report → record inputs and response.
  • Low-cost start: phone photos, spreadsheet/notebook and soil test through local agriculture services.
  • Measure: nutrient spend, crop response and cost per acre; do not expect the test alone to increase yield.

LOW → MID COST

Drip irrigation + filtration

Purpose: Apply water close to the root zone; potentially reduce water and labour waste when properly designed and maintained.

  • How: measure water source and plot → design crop-specific zones → install filter, mainline, laterals and emitters → flush lines → check uniformity → schedule using crop stage and soil moisture.
  • Low-cost start: ask for a design and quote for one pilot block, not a full farm package immediately.
  • Measure: water volume/hours, pump energy, labour, crop quality and yield against baseline.

LOW → MID COST

Soil-moisture sensor

Purpose: Observe moisture at the crop's active root depth to improve irrigation timing. One sensor represents only its installation zone, not every soil type in a large field.

  • How: place at representative root depth → calibrate/compare with manual soil checks → observe trends → decide irrigation → record water use and crop response.
  • Low-cost start: manual root-zone checks and a simple log; do not rely on a cheap garden probe for commercial irrigation control.
  • Indicative listed option: Fyllo Nero Infinity starts at ₹8,999 on the vendor site; verify current price, suitability and service.

MID / SHARED OR GROUP BUY

Farm weather station

Purpose: Local rainfall, humidity, temperature and wind observations can help schedule irrigation, field visits and spray windows—but forecasts and disease models are not guarantees.

  • How: install at a representative open site → maintain sensors → review readings with official forecast → act only when crop stage and field condition support it.
  • Low-cost start: IMD forecast, local rain gauge and shared station through an FPO/cooperative.
  • Indicative listed option: Fyllo Kairo is listed at ₹56,000; compare coverage, parameters, calibration, subscription and support.

MID / SERVICE BEFORE OWNERSHIP

Drone mapping / spraying

Purpose: Aerial imagery may help map variability; drone spraying may improve speed for suitable crops and approved products, but drift, calibration, regulation and operator competence matter.

  • How: define the exact job → check licensed operator and legal requirements → compare service quote with manual/mechanical alternatives → document coverage and result.
  • Low-cost start: hire a trained service provider for one defined task or explore FPO/custom-hiring access instead of buying a drone.
  • Do not use drone imagery alone to diagnose nutrient deficiency or disease.
Farm mechanisation portal ↗DGCA Digital Sky ↗
MID → HIGH COST

Automation, fertigation & farm IoT

Purpose: Timers, valves, flow meters and controllers can automate repetitive work when irrigation design, filtration and power supply are reliable.

  • How: first audit water pressure/flow → design irrigation zones → install manual bypass and filters → test fail-safe behaviour → train users → maintain records.
  • Low-cost start: zone valves, pressure gauge, flow meter and scheduled manual operation before adding cloud control.
  • Compare subscription, connectivity, calibration, repair, replacement and downtime costs.

04 / SELLING STRATEGY

Compare the net offer, not the headline price

Before harvest, ask each buyer for grade, quantity, weight method, deductions, packaging, transport responsibility, payment date, rejection rules and required paperwork. Keep quotes dated because prices move.

Export is a compliance and buyer problem—not simply a higher price.

Do not plant for export based on an unverified promise. Check the commodity and destination requirements with APEDA, the relevant plant quarantine/food-safety authorities and an experienced exporter. Export standards vary by commodity and country.

05 / WHERE TO BUY & HOW TO AVOID WASTED SPEND

Buy by specification, not by sales pitch

1. Request three written quotes

Compare the same specification, installation, tax, delivery, warranty, training, consumables, service response and annual subscription. Ask for total cost of ownership, not only device price.

2. Check government channels

Use the Government of India's farm mechanisation portal for machinery information and current scheme workflow. Confirm current eligibility and availability with the local Agriculture Department before assuming a subsidy.

Open farm mechanisation portal ↗

3. Ask for a field demo

Request a demonstration in a crop/soil similar to yours. Confirm sensor placement, calibration, connectivity, offline behaviour, maintenance, replacement cost and who interprets alerts.

4. Start with a small pilot

Define baseline measurements before installation. Record water, labour, inputs, yield, quality and costs. Scale only if the result is repeatable and the payback is reasonable.

5. Be careful with cheap soil meters

Low-cost garden probes may be useful for rough observation but are not a substitute for laboratory soil testing or calibrated commercial sensors. Do not use their NPK readings as a fertiliser prescription without validation.

6. Prefer hiring for expensive assets

For drone spraying, harvesters and specialist machinery, compare custom-hiring/FPO service cost with ownership, repair, licensing, utilisation and downtime. Low utilisation often makes hiring cheaper.
